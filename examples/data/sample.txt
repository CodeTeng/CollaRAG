Alfred Nobel was a Swedish chemist, engineer, inventor, and businessman. He is best known for inventing dynamite and for founding the Nobel Prize.

Nobel was born in Stockholm on October 21, 1833. His father, Immanuel Nobel, was an engineer and inventor who built bridges and buildings in Stockholm. Immanuel Nobel also experimented with different techniques for blasting rocks.

In 1842 the family moved to Saint Petersburg, where Immanuel started a successful mechanical workshop that supplied the Russian army. Young Alfred studied chemistry under Nikolai Zinin, who later introduced him to nitroglycerin.

Alfred Nobel invented dynamite in 1867. The patent was first registered in Sweden, and later extended to the United Kingdom and the United States. Dynamite made Nobel extremely wealthy, and the fortune he accumulated was later used to establish the Nobel Prizes.

The Nobel Prizes were first awarded in 1901, five years after Alfred Nobel's death in 1896. The prize categories include Physics, Chemistry, Physiology or Medicine, Literature, and Peace. The Peace Prize is awarded in Oslo, while the other prizes are awarded in Stockholm.
